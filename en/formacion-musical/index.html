Your music path in seven sessions

An introductory path for beginners and musical preparation. Spend 15–25 minutes per session at your own pace; repeat whenever needed. Share the link to practice with someone else.

1. Pulse and rhythm

Pulse is the regular reference; rhythm organizes sounds and silences against it. Walk four beats and clap on beats one and three. Keep the pulse when you stop clapping.

Goal: repeat two different patterns and review misses and early taps.

Open practice →

2. Read before playing

A note needs pitch and duration. Locate the clef and staff position, then count its length. Sing three neighboring notes before finding them on a keyboard. Start interactive reading with one clef.

Goal: explain a line and a space, and finish a session without rushing to guess.

Open practice →

3. Listen, sing and write

Listen to the whole phrase. Sing the reference note and describe whether the melody rises, falls or repeats. Enter notes afterward. During review, distinguish a wrong pitch from a wrong octave.

Goal: solve two different dictations and explain one mistake using the correct answer.

Open practice →

4. Chords and function

A triad combines root, third and fifth. Changing the lowest note can create an inversion without changing the chord. Compare I–IV–V–I and listen for an urge to continue; avoid reducing major and minor to a single emotion.

Goal: build a triad and first inversion, and describe which notes stay the same.

Open practice →

5. Create a motif

A motif is a short idea you can recognize when it returns. Write four positions, leave a rest and change only the ending on its repeat. Keep an initial version before transposing or reversing the contour.

Goal: create four original bars and save JSON; not every position needs a note.

Open practice →

6. From composition to mix

Send your composition to the lab. Listen before adding accompaniment. Adjust the instrument and change one effect at a time. Louder may seem better: compare at similar levels and keep a version without effects.

Goal: export WAV and explain one EQ and one spatial decision.

Open practice →

7. Share and review

Listen after a break. Separate your observations from automatic scores. Share a report and project with a teacher or peer using a channel you choose. This challenge does not publish work or send messages for you.

Goal: write what improved, what remains difficult and one specific question for feedback.
