Walamort · Listening journal

Your first mixing decisions

You do not need DAW experience. Try one practice at a time and describe a specific change. This guide does not play audio: open the laboratory in another tab to listen.

The laboratory is available in Spanish and English. This guide also supports Brazilian Portuguese.

Open laboratory in another tab

Before you start

Lower your device volume. In the laboratory press Reset defaults, choose Lightweight synthesis and start with the Walamort loop. Play loop starts sound; Stop pauses it. Keep the same output when comparing. If one version sounds louder, try RMS listening compensation: it is not a perceptual loudness measurement and is not exported.

Your journal

Your marks and notes stay in this browser, shared across this guide’s languages. Nothing is uploaded or graded automatically. Download a backup before clearing browser data.

1. EQ: brightness and quality are different decisions

An equalizer changes the level of frequency regions. Cutting highs can soften an attack; it does not remove an individual instrument from a mix.

  1. Reset defaults. Set Solo to Arpeggio to hear one track.
  2. Leave low and mid gain at 0 dB; set High shelf to −8 dB.
  3. Press Play loop and alternate A · Dry / B · Processed. A bypasses the rack and retains output and final protection.
  4. Describe brightness and clarity. Return High shelf to 0 dB and listen again.

What changed in the attack? When would you prefer each version?

2. Compression: watch attack and recovery

Threshold sets the level where compression acts; ratio controls the reduction. Attack and release change response speed. Makeup gain can make the result seem louder.

  1. Reset defaults. Choose Solo: Drums and the Strong compression preset.
  2. Play and observe compressor reduction. Compare attack at 0.005 s and 0.050 s, keeping other values fixed.
  3. Compare A/B and lower output if needed. Do not choose a version solely because it sounds louder.
  4. Stop and describe the initial hit and what happens between hits.

Which let more attack through? If you heard no difference, what would you try next?

3. Space: distinguish echo from reverb

Delay repeats sound; reverb produces a more diffuse tail. Both are parallel sends here. The reverb uses a synthetic response, not a recording of a real room.

  1. Reset defaults. Choose Solo: Arpeggio and keep Delay and Reverb enabled.
  2. Set Delay send to 20%, Feedback to 25% and Reverb send to 15%.
  3. Set each send to 0% separately. Listen for clear repeats versus a diffuse tail.
  4. Render WAV without playback and listen to the download. The extra tail is limited: a long echo may be truncated.

Which added useful depth? When did notes start to become obscured?
