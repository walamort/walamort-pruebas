Harmony lab

Build and hear the seven degrees of a key. Compare triads, seventh chords and inversions, and see which notes change. This explorer does not correct SATB harmonizations.

Use headphones. Sounds start when you press a button and stop when you leave the page.

Minor keys use the natural minor scale: the fifth-degree chord is minor. This is not the major dominant of harmonic minor.

Inversion

An inversion changes the bass while retaining the chord tones. In first inversion the third is in the bass; in second inversion, the fifth; in third inversion, the seventh.

In a major key, I–IV–V–I is a basic harmonic route. Listen to the tension of V returning to I. A seventh chord adds one more stacked third to the triad.
